====================================================================
  GREENOPS CI — WHAT IS THIS PROJECT AND HOW DOES IT WORK?
  (Written in simple, plain English)
====================================================================


--------------------------------------------------------------------
  THE PROBLEM IT SOLVES
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Every time a software team pushes new code, automated systems kick
in to check it — running tests, building the app, packaging it into
containers, and deploying it. These automated checks are called
"CI/CD pipelines" (CI = Continuous Integration, CD = Continuous
Delivery).

The problem? These pipelines run immediately, 24/7, no matter what
time of day it is or how dirty the electricity being used is.

Electricity is not equally clean at all hours. During the day when
the wind is blowing or the sun is shining, the electrical grid is
mostly powered by renewable energy (wind turbines, solar panels).
But in the evenings or during cold, still winter days, the grid
often falls back on gas or coal power stations — which release a
lot of carbon dioxide (CO2) into the atmosphere.

GreenOps CI is a tool that teaches your software pipelines to be
smarter about WHEN they run — choosing cleaner, greener moments
to do their heavy computing work, and pausing or waiting when the
grid is running mostly on fossil fuels.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
  THE CORE IDEA IN ONE SENTENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------

"Don't run your builds when the electricity is dirty —
 wait for the wind to pick up, then go full speed."


--------------------------------------------------------------------
  HOW DOES IT MEASURE "DIRTY" vs "CLEAN" ELECTRICITY?
--------------------------------------------------------------------

The tool connects to the National Grid ESO (Electricity System
Operator) in the UK, which publishes a live number every 30 minutes
called "carbon intensity". This number, measured in grams of CO2
per kilowatt-hour (gCO2/kWh), tells you how much carbon is being
released to generate each unit of electricity right now.

  - A LOW number (e.g. 65 gCO2/kWh)  = mostly wind and solar
  - A MEDIUM number (e.g. 150)        = mixed sources
  - A HIGH number (e.g. 300+)         = mostly gas and coal

The tool checks this number every few seconds and makes a decision.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
  THE THREE MODES (TRAFFIC LIGHT SYSTEM)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Based on the live carbon intensity number, the scheduler puts the
grid into one of three "modes" — just like a traffic light:

  GREEN  (below 100 gCO2/kWh)
  -------------------------------------------------------
  The grid is running cleanly. Renewable energy is high.
  → ALL pipelines run immediately at full speed.
  → Docker builds, test suites, deployments — all go.

  AMBER  (100 to 250 gCO2/kWh)
  -------------------------------------------------------
  The grid is a mix of clean and fossil-fuel generation.
  → Important, quick jobs (like fast unit tests) still run.
  → Big, heavy jobs (like building large Docker images) are
    slowed down or given lower priority to reduce emissions.

  RED    (above 250 gCO2/kWh)
  -------------------------------------------------------
  The grid is mostly fossil fuels right now.
  → Non-urgent pipelines are PAUSED and placed in a queue.
  → They wait until the carbon intensity drops back down.
  → Urgent hotfixes can still be "force dispatched" to bypass
    this rule if a team needs to push something critical.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
  THE 5 SCREENS IN THE DASHBOARD
--------------------------------------------------------------------

The project comes with a web application you can open in your
browser. It has five main screens:

  1. DASHBOARD (Home)
     The main screen. Shows the current carbon intensity as a
     big number (e.g. "142 gCO2/kWh"), a glowing traffic-light
     dot showing the current mode, and a live terminal at the
     bottom showing what the system is doing in real time.
     You can also click "Run Test CI Job Now" to trigger a
     sample pipeline and watch the carbon gate decision play out.

  2. PIPELINES & JOBS MANAGER
     A table showing all the recent CI/CD jobs — when they ran,
     what the carbon intensity was at that moment, whether they
     executed successfully or were deferred/paused, and how much
     CO2 was emitted vs. what it would have been without the tool.
     You can search, filter by mode (Green/Amber/Red), and view
     the logs for each job.

  3. ANALYTICS & CARBON SAVINGS
     Charts and numbers showing the total environmental impact
     of the tool over time. How many kilograms of CO2 were
     avoided? How many jobs ran during green windows vs. red?
     Equivalent impact shown in human-friendly terms like
     "equivalent to 2.2 trees working for a year" or
     "saved 121 miles of EV driving emissions."

  4. SIMULATION ROOM (Demo / Testing Mode)
     A control panel where you can manually pretend the carbon
     intensity is any number you want — using a slider or typing
     a value. This is useful for:
       - Demos: showing how the system would behave at 65 (green)
         vs 310 (red) without waiting for the real grid to change.
       - Testing: verifying your threshold settings actually work.
     You can click one-touch buttons like "Evening Gas Surge
     (310 gCO2/kWh)" or "High Offshore Wind (65 gCO2/kWh)" and
     instantly see how the pipeline scheduler would respond.

  5. SETTINGS
     Where you configure the system:
       - Change the Green/Amber/Red threshold numbers.
       - Switch the data source between the UK National Grid
         API and your own custom webhook/internal API.
       - Adjust how much power your CI runners consume (used
         to calculate CO2 emissions accurately).
       - Set the maximum time a job is allowed to wait before
         it gets dispatched regardless of grid status.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
  WHAT HAPPENS BEHIND THE SCENES WHEN A JOB IS TRIGGERED?
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Here is the step-by-step journey of a typical CI/CD pipeline job
going through the GreenOps system:

  Step 1: Code is pushed → pipeline trigger fires.

  Step 2: Before running anything, the scheduler checks the
          live UK grid carbon intensity number.

  Step 3: The number is compared against your thresholds:
            - Below 100?  → GREEN mode → dispatch now.
            - 100 to 250? → AMBER mode → dispatch with limits.
            - Above 250?  → RED mode → pause and queue.

  Step 4: If dispatched, the job runs on a CI runner (a small
          virtual machine in the cloud), and the energy used and
          CO2 emitted are calculated and logged.

  Step 5: If deferred (RED mode), the job waits in a queue.
          The system re-checks the grid every few minutes. When
          the intensity drops, the job is automatically released.

  Step 6: All results (mode, intensity at time of run, emissions,
          energy used, carbon saved vs baseline) are recorded in
          a CSV log file for auditing and reporting.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
  THE KEY FILES IN THIS PROJECT
--------------------------------------------------------------------

  scheduler/decide.py
    The "brain" — checks carbon intensity and decides Green,
    Amber, or Red mode.

  scheduler/config.yaml
    Your settings file — where you set the threshold numbers
    and configure the data provider.

  scheduler/estimate.py
    Does the maths — calculates how much energy (kWh) and
    carbon (gCO2) each pipeline job produces.

  scheduler/job_manager.py
    Manages the list of jobs — dispatching them, deferring
    them, logging them, and generating the analytics data.

  app/app.py
    The web server — handles requests from the browser and
    connects the dashboard to the scheduling logic.

  app/templates/index.html + app/static/js/app.js
    The dashboard itself — the five-screen web UI you see
    in the browser.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
  WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
--------------------------------------------------------------------

The software industry is one of the fastest-growing consumers of
electricity in the world. Data centers and CI/CD systems alone
account for a significant and growing share of global energy use.

Most of that compute is "dumb" — it runs whenever it's triggered,
regardless of whether the electricity powering it is clean or dirty.

GreenOps CI is a proof of concept that shows how easy it can be
to make software infrastructure "carbon-aware" — able to shift
workloads to times when renewable energy is plentiful, without
significantly impacting developer productivity.

The average delay for a deferred pipeline is about 18 minutes.
The average carbon saving is around 60% compared to always-on CI.

For large engineering organisations running thousands of pipelines
per day, this kind of time-shifting could save tonnes of CO2 per
year with almost no change to how developers write or ship code.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
  QUICK GLOSSARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------

  CI/CD Pipeline   A sequence of automated steps that test, build,
                   and deploy software whenever a developer commits
                   new code.

  Carbon Intensity The amount of CO2 produced per unit of
                   electricity, measured in gCO2/kWh. Lower = cleaner.

  gCO2/kWh         Grams of CO2 per kilowatt-hour. The unit used to
                   measure how dirty or clean electricity is at any
                   given moment.

  Deferral         When a pipeline job is intentionally paused and
                   placed in a waiting queue until grid emissions drop.

  Force Dispatch   Bypassing the carbon gate to run a job immediately
                   even during a red-alert period (for emergencies).

  Scope 2 Carbon   The indirect carbon emissions from the electricity
                   your organisation buys and consumes.

  Renewable Window A period when the grid is running mostly on clean
                   sources like wind, solar, or hydro power.
